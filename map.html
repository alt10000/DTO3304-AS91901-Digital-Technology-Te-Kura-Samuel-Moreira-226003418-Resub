Albania

Ruled in turn by Rome, Byzantium and the Ottomans, Albania declared independence in 1912. Its double-headed eagle recalls the medieval hero Skanderbeg.

Andorra

A tiny Pyrenean principality, co-ruled by a French and a Spanish-Catalan prince since 1278.

×

Austria

Seat of the Habsburgs, whose dynasty ruled a vast multinational empire in central Europe for over six centuries.

×

Belarus

Long divided between Lithuanian, Polish and Russian rule, Belarus became a Soviet republic before gaining independence in 1991.

×

Belgium

Independent from the Netherlands since 1830, Belgium sits where Latin and Germanic Europe meet and has been a battleground for centuries.

×

Bosnia and Herzegovina

A crossroads of Catholic, Orthodox and Muslim cultures, shaped by medieval kingdoms, Ottoman rule and Austro-Hungarian administration.

×

Britain

An island realm that grew from rival kingdoms into the United Kingdom, and later built the largest empire in history.

×

Bulgaria

One of Europe's oldest states, founded in 681. It built two medieval empires before centuries under Ottoman rule.

×

Croatia

A medieval kingdom on the Adriatic, later bound to Hungary and the Habsburgs for centuries.

×

Cyprus

An island at the crossroads of the eastern Mediterranean, ruled in turn by Byzantines, Venetians, Ottomans and the British.

×

Czechia

Once the Kingdom of Bohemia, a heartland of the Holy Roman Empire, and later part of Czechoslovakia.

×

Denmark

Home to one of the world's oldest monarchies, and once the head of the Kalmar Union that joined all of Scandinavia.

×

Estonia

Ruled in turn by Danes, Germans, Swedes and Russians, Estonia first won independence in 1918.

×

Finland

Part of Sweden for centuries, then a Russian grand duchy, Finland became independent in 1917.

×

France

A kingdom that became a republic and then an empire under Napoleon, shaping the politics of Europe for centuries.

×

Germany

within Germany itself is the history of countless duchies, petty kingdoms and old empires, all with their own flags and symbols to organize around and rally behind.

×

Greece

Birthplace of democracy and classical civilisation, later part of the Byzantine and Ottoman worlds before winning independence in the 1820s.

×

Hungary

Founded by the Magyars in the 9th century, Hungary grew into a great kingdom and later one half of Austria-Hungary.

×

Iceland

Settled by Norse seafarers in the 9th century, and home to the Althing, one of the oldest parliaments in the world.

×

Ireland

Once a land of rival kingdoms, then under English and British rule, Ireland won its independence in the early 1920s.

×

Italy

The heart of ancient Rome, later split into city-states, republics and kingdoms until unification in 1861.

×

Kosovo

A region with deep medieval Serbian and Albanian history, Kosovo declared independence in 2008.

×

Latvia

Ruled by Germanic knights, Poles, Swedes and Russians before declaring independence in 1918.

×

Liechtenstein

A tiny Alpine principality created in 1719 and named after its ruling dynasty, a rare survivor of the Holy Roman Empire.

×

Lithuania

The heart of the vast Grand Duchy of Lithuania, which once stretched from the Baltic almost to the Black Sea.

×

Luxembourg

A small, once heavily fortified state that is today the only grand duchy left in the world.

×

Malta

A strategic Mediterranean island ruled by Phoenicians, Romans, the Knights of St John and the British.

×

Moldova

Once part of the medieval Principality of Moldavia, later divided between Ottoman, Romanian and Russian influence.

×

Monaco

A tiny coastal principality that has been ruled by the Grimaldi family since 1297.

×

Montenegro

A mountain stronghold that resisted Ottoman rule for centuries and became a kingdom in 1910.

×

Netherlands

Won its freedom from Spain in the 1500s and rose to become a wealthy maritime republic and global trading power.

×

North Macedonia

A crossroads of Byzantine, Bulgarian, Serbian and Ottoman history, it became independent from Yugoslavia in 1991.

×

Norway

Home of the Viking kingdoms, later united with Denmark and then Sweden before gaining full independence in 1905.

×

Poland

Once part of the vast Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth, then partitioned and erased from the map for 123 years until 1918.

×

Portugal

One of Europe's oldest nation-states, whose explorers opened sea routes to Africa, Asia and Brazil.

×

Romania

Formed from the union of Wallachia and Moldavia in 1859, with roots reaching back to Roman Dacia.

×

Russia

From medieval Rus' to the empire of the tsars, it grew over the centuries into the largest country on Earth.

×

San Marino

Traditionally founded in 301 AD and claimed to be the oldest surviving republic in the world.

×

Serbia

A medieval kingdom and empire, later under Ottoman rule, that won its independence in the 19th century.

×

Slovakia

Long part of the Kingdom of Hungary, then of Czechoslovakia, Slovakia has been independent since 1993.

×

Slovenia

Ruled by the Habsburgs for centuries, then part of Yugoslavia, Slovenia has been independent since 1991.

×

Spain

Formed from rival medieval kingdoms, Spain grew into a vast global empire after 1492.

×

Sweden

A Baltic power that dominated northern Europe during the 17th century.

×

Switzerland

Grew from a pact between Alpine communities in 1291 into a neutral confederation of cantons.

×

Ukraine

The heartland of medieval Kyivan Rus', later divided among Poland, Lithuania, Russia and Austria-Hungary.

Vatican City

The world's smallest state, seat of the papacy and last remnant of the old Papal States.
