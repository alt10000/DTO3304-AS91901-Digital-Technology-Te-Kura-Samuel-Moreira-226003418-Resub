Moldova

Overview

Once part of the medieval Principality of Moldavia, later divided between Ottoman, Romanian and Russian influence.

Historical Flags

State flag of the Principality of Moldavia (14th–19th centuries)

The principality's best-known historical banner was a red flag bearing the golden head of an aurochs, the wild ancestor of domestic cattle. The aurochs' head was the central emblem of Moldavian heraldry and was associated with the founding traditions of the principality. Some surviving historical depictions also show Saint George slaying the dragon on the reverse. Medieval Moldavia did not have one continuously standardised state flag in the modern sense; its rulers used different princely and military standards over the centuries.

State flag of the Moldavian Democratic Republic (1917–1918)

The flag was a horizontal blue-yellow-red tricolour, with the republic's coat of arms in the centre of the yellow stripe. The upper blue stripe carried the inscription Republica Democratică Moldovenească și Independentă (“Moldavian Democratic and Independent Republic”). The design was associated with Bessarabian heraldic colours and was used during the republic's brief existence following the Russian Revolution.

State flag of the Moldavian Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic (1925–1932)

The flag featured a red field with the yellow Cyrillic abbreviation Р.А.С.С.М. (RASSM) arranged in an arc near the upper-left corner. Below it were the hammer and sickle, accompanied by agricultural imagery including maize, wheat and grapevines. The flag represented the Moldavian ASSR, established in 1924 as an autonomous republic within the Ukrainian Soviet Socialist Republic.

State flag of the Moldavian Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic (1937–January 1938)

This version retained the red background and Soviet symbolism but changed the arrangement of the inscriptions. The gold hammer and sickle appeared above the abbreviation УРСР (Ukrainian SSR in Ukrainian), with RSSU below it, representing the Ukrainian SSR in Romanian-language initials. The flag reflected the republic's status as an autonomous territory within Soviet Ukraine.

State flag of the Moldavian Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic (January 1938–1940)

The flag was based on the previous design, retaining the red field and gold hammer and sickle. Its inscriptions were changed to include the republic's name in Ukrainian and Moldovan. The Moldovan text initially appeared in Latin script, but was changed to Cyrillic in February 1938 following the reintroduction of Cyrillic for written Moldovan. The flag remained in use until the Moldavian ASSR was abolished in August 1940.

State flag of the Moldavian Soviet Socialist Republic (1940–1952)

Following the creation of the Moldavian SSR in August 1940, its flag consisted of a red field with the gold Cyrillic abbreviation РССМ (RSSM, Moldavian Soviet Socialist Republic) above a gold hammer and sickle in the upper-left corner. The design was formalised in the republic's 1941 Constitution and remained in use until January 1952.

State flag of the Moldavian Soviet Socialist Republic (1952–1990)

Adopted on 31 January 1952, this flag featured three horizontal stripes in a red-green-red arrangement, with the green stripe running through the centre. The proportions of the stripes were 3:2:3. A gold hammer and sickle surmounted by a red, gold-bordered star appeared in the upper-left corner. The reverse side officially omitted the Soviet emblem.

State flag of the Moldavian Soviet Socialist Republic, later known as SSR Moldova (1990)

During the national independence movement, the traditional Romanian-style blue-yellow-red vertical tricolour was restored on 27 April 1990. Initially, the design was adopted without a coat of arms; a version incorporating the state emblem on the central yellow stripe was subsequently established. The tricolour replaced the Soviet red-and-green flag and became the basis of the modern Moldovan flag.

State flag of the Republic of Moldova (1990–2010)

Following the country's declaration of independence on 27 August 1991, the blue-yellow-red vertical tricolour continued as the national state flag. The national coat of arms, featuring a golden aurochs' head on a shield placed on an eagle's breast, appeared in the centre of the yellow stripe. The design was officially regulated by the Parliament's decision of 12 May 1990 and retained after independence.

National flag and ensign of Moldova (2010–present)

The modern flag retains the vertical blue-yellow-red tricolour and the national coat of arms in the centre. Under the flag law adopted on 17 September 2010, the coat of arms is displayed on both sides of the flag, with the reverse showing a mirrored version. The coat of arms depicts an eagle holding a cross, an olive branch and a sceptre, with the traditional aurochs' head displayed on its breast.

Historical note: Moldova's flag history is strongly connected to the aurochs, an animal that became the central symbol of the medieval Principality of Moldavia. The blue, yellow and red colours also have historical roots in the heraldry of Moldavia and Bessarabia. The modern tricolour was restored during the national revival of 1990 and became the flag of the independent Republic of Moldova in 1991.

Historical clarification: The medieval Moldavian banners were princely and military standards rather than continuously standardised national flags. The three Moldavian ASSR entries represent distinct historical designs and inscription changes, although the 1937–1938 version was used only briefly. The 1990 tricolour also went through transitional stages, first appearing without the coat of arms before the emblem was incorporated into the state flag. The 2010 law standardised the modern flag's two-sided coat-of-arms design.
