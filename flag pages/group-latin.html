Latin and Romance Peoples

About the Latin and Romance group

The Romance peoples live in lands where Latin developed into French, Italian, Spanish, Portuguese, Romanian, Catalan and related languages. On this site the group includes France, Italy, Spain, Portugal, Romania, Moldova, Andorra, Monaco, San Marino and Vatican City.

Their flags fall into two broad families: tricolours inspired by the French Revolution and older flags built from royal or dynastic arms.

Flags side by side

Revolutionary tricolours

The French tricolour of the 1790s inspired republicans well beyond France. Italian republics adopted green, white and red in 1797, and Romanian revolutionaries took up their own vertical tricolour in the 1840s. Each used the French pattern to express liberty and national identity.

Three near-twins

Romania, Moldova and Andorra all fly blue, yellow and red in vertical stripes. The flags are told apart mainly by their coats of arms and proportions: Romania’s is plain, Moldova’s carries an eagle with an aurochs’ head, and Andorra’s shows four historic quarters.

The Iberian pair

Spain and Portugal grew from medieval crowns and took their flags from them. Spain’s red and yellow come from the arms of the old Crown of Aragon and Castile, while Portugal’s green and red replaced the blue and white of the monarchy in 1910.

Small states, local symbols

Monaco, San Marino and Vatican City have flags that come from their own rulers and institutions rather than from revolution. The red and white of Monaco are the Grimaldi colours, San Marino’s white and blue are linked to its three towers, and the Vatican’s yellow and white refer to the keys of Saint Peter.
