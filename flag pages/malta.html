Flag of the Order of Saint John used in Malta (1530–1798)

The Order's flag featured a red field with a large white cross. It represented the Knights Hospitaller, who governed Malta from 1530 until Napoleon's French forces captured the islands in 1798. The white cross became strongly associated with the Order and Malta's history.

British Malta state ensign (1813–1875)

An early British-era Maltese ensign is depicted as a red flag featuring the Union Flag in the upper-left canton and a white Cross of St George. However, the historical evidence for this particular design is uncertain: surviving flag references from the nineteenth century are inconsistent, and it may not have been an officially authorised state ensign throughout this period.

British Malta state ensign (1875–c.1898)

This design used a blue field with the Union Flag in the canton and a colonial badge in the fly. The badge featured a white Maltese cross above a vertically divided white-and-red panel, surrounded by an ornate decorative border. It reflected Malta's British colonial status while retaining a symbol associated with the island's former Hospitaller rulers.

British Malta state ensign (c.1898–1923)

Around 1898 the colonial badge on the Blue Ensign was redesigned as a white-and-red shield, dropping the Maltese cross. Tradition says the red and white date from 1091, when Count Roger of Sicily gave Malta’s people a piece of his red-and-white banner.

British Malta state ensign (1923–1943)

In 1923 the white disc was dropped from the badge, leaving the red-and-white shield on the plain blue field beneath the Union Flag. This was the ensign Malta flew in the early years of the Second World War, including the siege that earned the island the George Cross in 1942.

British Malta state ensign (1943–1964)

Following the award of the George Cross to the people of Malta in 1942, King George VI authorised its inclusion in the colonial badge on 28 December 1943. The blue ensign consequently displayed a white-and-red shield with a blue canton containing the George Cross, all placed on a white disc. The design remained in use until Malta became independent in 1964.

National flag of Malta (1964–present)

Upon independence on 21 September 1964, Malta adopted a white-and-red vertical bicolour, with white at the hoist and red at the fly. The George Cross, awarded in recognition of the Maltese people's bravery during the Second World War, appears in the upper-left corner of the white stripe, outlined in red. The flag's design was based on the island's earlier civil flag, with the blue background behind the George Cross removed.

Historical note: Malta's flag history reflects three major periods: the rule of the Knights Hospitaller, British colonial administration, and independent statehood. The white and red colours have long been associated with Malta, while the George Cross commemorates the island's resistance and the bravery of its population during the Second World War.

Historical clarification: The 1813 ensign should be treated cautiously because its precise design and official status are not firmly established. Also, the George Cross was awarded in 1942, but it was not incorporated into the colonial badge until the Royal Warrant of 28 December 1943. Malta's modern national flag dates from independence in 1964; it is distinct from the British-era Blue Ensign.
