Oriflamme of Charlemagne (traditionally 8th–9th centuries)

The Oriflamme was a sacred military standard traditionally associated with Charlemagne and later adopted by the French kings. It was reportedly a red banner, sometimes depicted with golden flames or tassels, and became a symbol of the French monarchy and the royal army; its association with Charlemagne is largely legendary.

Banner of the Kingdom of France (10th–12th centuries)

Early French royal banners are not well documented, and surviving depictions are largely later reconstructions. The blue field and golden fleur-de-lis became associated with the French monarchy during the early Capetian period, although the precise appearance of the earliest banners is uncertain.

Banner of the Kingdom of France (12th–13th centuries)

This banner featured a blue field covered with numerous golden fleurs-de-lis, known heraldically as France Ancient. The fleur-de-lis became an established symbol of French royal authority during the reigns of the early Capetian kings.

Banner of the Kingdom of France (14th–16th centuries)

The design featured a blue field with three large golden fleurs-de-lis, arranged two above and one below. Known as France Modern, this simplified version was introduced during the reign of Charles V in the 14th century and became the familiar emblem of the French monarchy.

Flag of the Kingdom of France and the Bourbon Restoration (1365–1792; 1814–1815; 1815–1830)

The three golden fleurs-de-lis on blue are usually credited to Charles V, who simplified the earlier scatter of lilies in the 14th century. The flag flew until the Revolution ended the monarchy in 1792, and returned during the Bourbon Restoration of 1814–15 and 1815–30, when it became a symbol for royalists.

State Flag of the Kingdom of France under the Absolute Monarchy (1715–1789)

This elaborate royal standard featured a white field decorated with golden fleurs-de-lis and a central royal coat of arms, surrounded by the collars of the Orders of Saint Michael and the Holy Spirit. It was used as a royal standard, particularly when the king was present, rather than as a general national flag.

Flag of the Kingdom of France and the French First Republic (1790–1794)

The first revolutionary-era French flag consisted of a white field with a blue, white and red tricolour in the upper-left corner. It was introduced during the early French Revolution, combining the traditional royal white with the colours of Paris, before being replaced by the familiar vertical tricolour in 1794.

Royal Flag of the Kingdom of France (1814–1830)

Following Napoleon's defeat, the Bourbon monarchy restored the traditional white royal flag, decorated with golden fleurs-de-lis. It replaced the revolutionary tricolour during the Restoration, although the tricolour returned after the July Revolution of 1830.

Flag of the French Second Republic (1848)

During the February Revolution of 1848, republican revolutionaries and radical demonstrators promoted a plain red flag as an alternative to the traditional blue-white-red tricolour. The provisional government ultimately retained the tricolour, so the red flag was a revolutionary symbol rather than the Republic's official national flag.

Flag of Free France (1940–1944)

The Free French movement under General Charles de Gaulle continued using the blue-white-red tricolour, distinguished by the red Cross of Lorraine. Adopted as the movement's emblem in 1940, the cross symbolised resistance to Nazi Germany and opposition to the Vichy regime.

Flag of France (1976–2020)

This version of the French tricolour featured a lighter shade of blue than the traditional navy blue. Introduced during the presidency of Valéry Giscard d'Estaing, it was intended to harmonise the French flag's colours with those of the European flag.

Flag of France (2020–present)

The current version uses a darker navy blue, returning to a shade closer to the original revolutionary-era design. President Emmanuel Macron introduced the change in 2020, although it became publicly known in 2021; the flag retains the familiar vertical blue, white and red stripes.

Historical note: France's flag history reflects the country's changing political systems, from medieval royal banners bearing golden fleurs-de-lis to the revolutionary tricolour. The blue-white-red design, first introduced during the French Revolution, became the enduring national symbol despite temporary interruptions during the Bourbon Restoration.
