Vatican City

Historical Flags

The flags of the Papacy developed from medieval banners representing the authority of the pope and the Holy Roman Church into the yellow-and-white flag of the modern Vatican City State. For much of its history, the Papal States used several different flags for military, naval, ceremonial and civil purposes rather than one uniform national flag. Amazon Web Services, Inc.

Banner of Pope Boniface VIII (1294–1303)

A crimson-red, swallow-tailed banner bearing two crossed golden keys beneath or in front of a golden papal canopy, known as an umbraculum. It represented the spiritual and temporal authority of the papacy. Boniface VIII's pontificate is also associated with the development of the ceremonial Banner of the Holy Roman Church, which was carried during papal processions and military campaigns.

Banner of Pope Leo X (1513–1521)

An orange swallow-tailed banner decorated with seven red roundels. It was a personal papal banner associated with Leo X, a member of the Medici family, rather than a general flag of the Papal States. Its colours and design reflected the heraldic and ceremonial traditions of the Renaissance papacy.

Banner of Pope Paul III (1534–1549)

A dark-red swallow-tailed banner displaying the papal tiara above the crossed keys of Saint Peter. It represented the authority of Paul III and the Holy See during a period marked by the Catholic Reformation and the Council of Trent. Like the banners of earlier popes, it was a papal standard rather than a national flag.

Pilot flag, infantry colours and de facto civil flag (1808–1870)

A vertically divided yellow-and-white bicolour. The colours were introduced by Pope Pius VII in 1808, when he ordered papal troops that remained loyal to him to adopt yellow-and-white cockades, distinguishing them from forces incorporated into Napoleon's army. The colours subsequently appeared on military standards, pilot flags and civil decorations, although their use was not yet governed by one uniform national-flag design.

War and proto-national flag flown over Porta Pia during the Fall of Rome (1870)

This yellow-and-white flag with the crossed keys beneath the papal tiara is associated with the defence of Rome on 20 September 1870, when Italian forces broke through at Porta Pia and ended the Papal States’ rule. The Pope refused to accept the loss and continued to claim sovereignty, which led to the stand-off resolved only in 1929.

National and state flag of Vatican City (1929–2001)

Vatican City became an independent state under the Lateran Treaty of 11 February 1929, and its flag was first hoisted on 8 June 1929. The yellow and white recall the gold and silver keys of Saint Peter, shown crossed beneath the papal tiara, which stand for the power to bind and loose on earth and in heaven.

National and state flag of Vatican City (2001–2023)

The Fundamental Law promulgated by Pope John Paul II in November 2000 and in force from early 2001 set out the state’s flag and arms in law. The yellow-and-white design with the crossed keys and tiara was kept, so the change was one of legal wording rather than appearance.

National and state flag of Vatican City (2023–present)

A new Fundamental Law promulgated by Pope Francis on 13 May 2023 and effective from 7 June 2023 again defined the flag and arms of Vatican City State. The yellow-and-white bicolour with crossed keys and tiara was retained, with only the legal description updated.
