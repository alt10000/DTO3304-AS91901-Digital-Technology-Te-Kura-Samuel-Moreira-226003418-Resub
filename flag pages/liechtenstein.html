Principality of Liechtenstein (1719–1852)

The Principality was created on 23 January 1719 when Emperor Charles VI united Vaduz and Schellenberg under the Liechtenstein family. The flag used the gold and red of the princely house’s arms, so it served as a dynastic banner rather than a modern national flag.

Principality of Liechtenstein (1852–1921)

In 1852 the flag was changed to blue and red, colours the princely household already used and which are popularly said to stand for the sky and the evening hearth fires. Its orientation was not always consistent in this period.

Principality of Liechtenstein (1921–1937)

The 1921 Constitution turned Liechtenstein into a constitutional monarchy with popular participation, and it fixed the flag as plain blue over red. This was the first legal definition of the national colours, but the plain design turned out to be identical to Haiti’s flag.

Principality of Liechtenstein (1937–1982)

A golden princely hat was added in the upper-left portion of the blue stripe on 24 June 1937. The change followed the discovery at the 1936 Berlin Olympic Games that Liechtenstein's plain blue-and-red flag was almost identical to the flag of Haiti. The hat distinguished the two designs and highlighted the country's monarchy.

National flag and ensign of Liechtenstein (1982–present)

A law of 30 June 1982 gave the princely hat a more modern outline and set its place in the corner of the blue stripe. The crown, added in 1937, stands for the prince and is the feature that sets the flag apart from Haiti’s.
