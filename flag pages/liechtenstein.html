Liechtenstein

Overview

A tiny Alpine principality created in 1719 and named after its ruling dynasty, a rare survivor of the Holy Roman Empire.

Historical Flags

Principality of Liechtenstein (1719–1852)

When the Principality of Liechtenstein was established in 1719, its flag was a horizontal bicolour of gold (yellow) over red. These colours were derived from the heraldic colours of the Princely House of Liechtenstein.

Principality of Liechtenstein (1852–1921)

The gold-and-red design was replaced by the national colours blue and red. The flag is commonly depicted with two vertical stripes, blue at the hoist and red at the fly, although historical sources indicate that the orientation was not always consistent. The colours had been associated with the princely household before their adoption for the flag.

Principality of Liechtenstein (1921–1937)

The Constitution of 5 October 1921 formally established a horizontal bicolour of blue over red. This was the first clearly prescribed version of the modern blue-and-red national flag, and it had no emblem.

Principality of Liechtenstein (1937–1982)

A golden princely hat was added in the upper-left portion of the blue stripe on 24 June 1937. The change followed the discovery at the 1936 Berlin Olympic Games that Liechtenstein's plain blue-and-red flag was almost identical to the flag of Haiti. The hat distinguished the two designs and highlighted the country's monarchy.

National flag and ensign of Liechtenstein (1982–present)

The current flag retains the blue-over-red horizontal bicolour and golden princely hat, but the hat's design was modernised by legislation on 30 June 1982. The hat appears in the upper-left corner of the blue stripe, towards the flagpole. The design is used as Liechtenstein's national flag and civil ensign.

Historical note: The blue and red colours originated in the livery and military colours of the Princely House, rather than being newly invented in 1852. The country's official historical account records blue-and-red clothing among the prince's servants from around the turn of the eighteenth century, and a blue-and-red military standard design from 1793.

Historical clarification: The 1937 change was prompted by the similarity between Liechtenstein's flag and Haiti's flag at the 1936 Olympics. The golden emblem is more accurately called a princely hat (Fürstenhut) than a conventional crown. The 1982 change modernised its appearance rather than replacing the flag's basic design.
