Germanic Peoples

About the Germanic group

The Germanic peoples of Europe include the German-speaking lands and the Dutch-speaking Netherlands, together with Luxembourg and Liechtenstein. The Nordic countries share Germanic roots too, and have their own page.

Their flags are a mixture of horizontal tricolours, which became common after the French Revolution, and older flags tied to emperors, princes and cantons.

Flags side by side

Horizontal tricolours

Germany, the Netherlands, Luxembourg and Austria all use horizontal bands. Luxembourg’s flag looks very like the Dutch one, differing mainly in a lighter blue. The Russian tricolour is often said to have been inspired by the Dutch flag after Peter the Great visited the Netherlands.

The imperial eagle

Many Germanic states used the eagle as a sign of imperial authority. The Holy Roman Empire carried a double-headed black eagle on gold, Prussia used a crowned single-headed eagle, and Austria revived the double-headed eagle in the 1930s.

Small and different

Switzerland and Liechtenstein have flags that break from the tricolour pattern. Switzerland’s square flag grew out of a military banner, and Liechtenstein added a crown in 1937 after the 1936 Olympics showed its plain blue-and-red flag matched Haiti’s.

Colours with long histories

Black, red and gold started as the uniform colours of volunteers in the Napoleonic wars before becoming a symbol of German unity in 1848. Austria’s red and white are traditionally linked to Duke Leopold V in 1191, though that story is a legend.
