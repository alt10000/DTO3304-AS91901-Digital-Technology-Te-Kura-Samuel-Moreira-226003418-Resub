Belgium

Overview

Independent from the Netherlands since 1830, Belgium sits where Latin and Germanic Europe meet and has been a battleground for centuries.

Historical Flags

Flag of the Spanish Netherlands (1482–1714)

During Spanish Habsburg rule, the principal flag of the Spanish Netherlands featured a white field bearing the red Cross of Burgundy, a symbol associated with the House of Burgundy and its territories. A separate civil ensign also existed, featuring horizontal red, white and yellow stripes with the Cross of Burgundy in the centre.

Flag of the Austrian Netherlands (1714–1797)

Following the transfer of the Southern Netherlands to Austrian Habsburg rule, the territory adopted a horizontal tricolour of red, white and gold, incorporating the Cross of Burgundy. In 1781, the design was modified by replacing the cross with the black double-headed eagle of the Habsburg monarchy, reflecting its Austrian sovereignty.

Flag of the United Belgian States (1789–1790)

Adopted during the Brabant Revolution against Austrian rule, this flag featured three horizontal stripes of red, black and yellow, inspired by the colours of the Duchy of Brabant. It represented the short-lived United Belgian States, which declared independence in January 1790 before being reconquered by Austrian forces later that year.

Flag of the Belgian Revolution and First Belgian Flag (1830–1831)

Introduced during the Belgian Revolution in August 1830, this flag featured three horizontal stripes of black, yellow and red, drawing inspiration from the colours of the Brabant Revolution. It became the first national flag of independent Belgium and initially flew with its stripes arranged horizontally before being changed to a vertical arrangement in January 1831.

Modern Flag of Belgium (1831–present)

Officially established in October 1831, the Belgian flag features three vertical stripes of black, yellow and red, with black positioned at the hoist. Its colours originate from the historic coat of arms of the Duchy of Brabant and symbolise Belgium's independence, national identity and revolutionary heritage.

Historical note: The Belgian national colours originated from the Duchy of Brabant and were revived during both the Brabant Revolution of 1789 and the Belgian Revolution of 1830. Although the two revolutionary flags shared the same colours, their stripe arrangements differed: the United Belgian States used horizontal stripes, while the modern Belgian flag uses vertical stripes.
