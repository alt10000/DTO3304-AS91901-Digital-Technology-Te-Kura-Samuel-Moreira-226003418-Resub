North Macedonia

Overview

A crossroads of Byzantine, Bulgarian, Serbian and Ottoman history, it became independent from Yugoslavia in 1991.

Historical Flags

North Macedonia's flag history reflects the region's revolutionary movements, the establishment of Macedonian state institutions during the Second World War, and the country's transition from Yugoslav republic to independent state. Some early flags in this history are disputed or were adopted as national symbols only retrospectively.

Flag of the 3rd Cheta of Pitu Guli, later associated with the Republic of Kruševo (1903)

This revolutionary banner is described as a red flag embroidered in gold, featuring two hands clasped together above a palm wreath, with a torch above them. An inscription around the design read “Smrt ili sloboda — Zname na Krushovskata cheta” (“Death or Freedom — Flag of the Kruševo Squad”). It was associated with the detachment commanded by Pitu Guli during the Ilinden Uprising of August 1903. It later became popularly associated with the short-lived Republic of Kruševo, although it is not conclusively established as the republic's official state flag.

Flag of the Macedonian Colony in Petrograd (1914)

Designed by members of the Macedonian Scientific and Literary Society in St Petersburg, this flag had a red field with a white canton containing a red unicorn, traditionally interpreted as Alexander the Great's horse Bucephalus. In the lower fly was a golden quarter-sun with rays and the inscription “Едина независима Македония” (“One Independent Macedonia”). Published in the society's journal Makedonskiy Golos in 1914, it was an early proposal for an independent Macedonian state's flag. The original flag is preserved in the Museum of Macedonia.

Historical flag of the Internal Macedonian Revolutionary Organization (1920s–present)

The flag associated with the post-First World War Internal Macedonian Revolutionary Organization (IMRO) is a horizontal bicolour of red over black. It was adopted in the early 1920s under the organisation's leadership and became associated with its revolutionary movement and aspirations for an independent Macedonia. It is important to distinguish this flag from the various banners used by revolutionary groups during the earlier Ilinden Uprising.

Flag of the Yugoslav Partisan movement (1941–1945)

The Yugoslav Partisans generally used a horizontal blue-white-red tricolour with a red five-pointed star in the centre. The star symbolised the communist-led resistance against the Axis occupation and became the movement's principal identifying emblem. Macedonian partisan units also used red flags, sometimes bearing a gold-edged red star, as well as unit-specific inscriptions and symbols.

Flag of the First Macedonian Brigade (1944)

This military banner consisted of a red field with the brigade's name written in yellow Cyrillic lettering across its lower portion. It represented the First Macedonian Brigade during the Second World War and symbolised the Macedonian partisan forces fighting against Axis occupation. Red flags bearing military-unit names became important identifying standards for the partisan formations.

Flag of Democratic Federal Macedonia (1944–1946)

Established during the first session of the Anti-Fascist Assembly for the National Liberation of Macedonia (ASNOM) on 2 August 1944, the flag featured a red field with a red five-pointed star outlined in gold in the centre. The design drew on the red flags of the partisan movement and represented the newly established Macedonian federal state within Yugoslavia.

Flag of the People's Republic of Macedonia (1946–1963) and Socialist Republic of Macedonia (1963–1991)

The flag retained its red field but moved the gold-outlined red five-pointed star from the centre to the upper-left corner. Its design was formally established in the first Constitution of the People's Republic of Macedonia on 31 December 1946. The flag continued in use after the republic was renamed the Socialist Republic of Macedonia in 1963.

Flag of the Republic of Macedonia (1992–1995)

Following independence from Yugoslavia in 1991, the country adopted a red flag featuring a large golden-yellow sixteen-rayed sun, commonly known as the Vergina Sun. The symbol was inspired by an ancient Macedonian emblem discovered at the archaeological site of Vergina in Greece. Its adoption caused a dispute with Greece over the use of the ancient symbol, and the flag was replaced in 1995 following the Interim Accord between the two countries.

Flag of North Macedonia (1995–present)

Adopted on 5 October 1995, this flag features a red field with a golden-yellow sun in the centre and eight broad rays extending towards the edges. The rays spread horizontally, vertically and diagonally, creating a distinctive design. It was designed by Miroslav Grčev, with modifications to the final arrangement by Kostadin Tanchev-Dinkata. The flag replaced the Vergina Sun design following the agreement with Greece.

Historical note: North Macedonia's modern flag traditions are strongly connected to the red revolutionary banners of the Ilinden Uprising and the partisan movement of the Second World War. The red field and golden-yellow sun became recurring symbols of Macedonian statehood, appearing in different forms on the flags of Democratic Federal Macedonia, the independent Republic of Macedonia and today's North Macedonia.

Historical clarification: The 1903 banner associated with Pitu Guli's detachment should not automatically be treated as the formally adopted national flag of the Republic of Kruševo. Historical accounts describe several different revolutionary banners during the Ilinden Uprising, and the precise flag flown over Kruševo is not conclusively established. Similarly, the red-and-black IMRO flag dates from the organisation's post-First World War period, rather than being a securely documented flag of the original organisation founded in 1893. The 1944 partisan and state flags also had several variations, including versions with the star in the centre or in the upper-left corner.
