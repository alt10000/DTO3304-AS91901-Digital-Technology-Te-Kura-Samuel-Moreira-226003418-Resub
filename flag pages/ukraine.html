Historical Flags

The history of Ukrainian flags spans medieval princely heraldry, the military banners of the Cossack Hetmanate, the independence movements of 1917–1921, and the Soviet period. The blue-and-yellow national flag emerged as a modern national symbol in the 19th century and was officially adopted by independent Ukraine in 1992. Some medieval and Cossack designs are reconstructions, since these earlier states did not have standardised national flags in the modern sense.

Flag of Kyivan Rus (9th–13th centuries)

Kyivan Rus did not have one universally standardised state flag. Its rulers and military forces used banners in different colours, with red particularly common and individual princely standards sometimes bearing a golden trident or bident. Any single flag shown as the flag of Kyivan Rus is therefore a modern reconstruction rather than a securely documented national design.

Flag of the Kingdom of Galicia–Volhynia (1119–1349)

Commonly reconstructed as an azure-blue banner bearing a golden lion, the heraldic emblem associated with the rulers of Galicia. The lion later became an important symbol of western Ukrainian identity and was used in the heraldry of Lviv and the West Ukrainian People's Republic. The date 1119 is used in some flag chronologies, but the banner is better understood as a reconstruction from medieval heraldic evidence.

Flag of the Cossack Hetmanate (17th–18th centuries)

The Hetmanate did not have one fixed flag throughout its existence. Its banners varied in colour and design, with crimson and red particularly prominent. Hetman standards could feature a white Archangel Michael on red or the personal coat of arms of the hetman; regimental and company flags also displayed religious imagery and the Cossack with a musket. By the 18th century, blue banners with golden crosses, stars and other emblems were increasingly common.

Flag of the Ukrainian People's Republic (unofficial, 1917–1918)

During the Ukrainian national movement of 1917, both blue-over-yellow and yellow-over-blue flags appeared at demonstrations and military gatherings. The yellow-over-blue arrangement is often shown in historical flag collections as an unofficial early version, but contemporary accounts differ about which order was most commonly used. The colours became a powerful symbol of Ukrainian national independence.

Flag of the Ukrainian People's Republic (1918–1921)

The national flag was a horizontal blue-and-yellow bicolour. The Central Rada formally adopted the national colours in March 1918, although the precise legal history and the order of the colours have been debated by historians. The flag was used by the Ukrainian People's Republic and continued under the Directory during the Ukrainian War of Independence.

Flag of the West Ukrainian People's Republic and the Ukrainian State (1918–1920)

Both governments used the blue-over-yellow bicolour. The West Ukrainian People's Republic adopted it in November 1918, while the Ukrainian State under Hetman Pavlo Skoropadsky also used the blue-and-yellow national colours. The Ukrainian State's military and naval flags included additional heraldic and service emblems.

Flag of the Ukrainian Soviet Socialist Republic (1919–1929)

The first Soviet Ukrainian flag was red, with the gold Cyrillic initials У.С.С.Р. in the upper-left area. It was adopted in March 1919 and represented the Ukrainian Socialist Soviet Republic. It replaced the national colours with the revolutionary red banner of the Bolshevik government.

Flag of the Ukrainian Soviet Socialist Republic (1929–1937)

The red field and gold initials were retained, but the lettering was changed to У.С.Р.Р., reflecting the Ukrainian-language name of the republic. A gold border was also added around the canton in the 1930s.

Flag of the Ukrainian Soviet Socialist Republic (1937–1950)

The red flag was revised to display the gold hammer and sickle above the gold Cyrillic abbreviation УРСР. The lettering reflected the revised Ukrainian name of the republic. This design remained in use until the adoption of a new flag with a blue lower stripe in 1950.

Flag of the Ukrainian Soviet Socialist Republic (1950–1991)

The flag consisted of a red upper field and an azure-blue lower stripe, which occupied one-third of the flag's height. A gold hammer and sickle and a red, gold-bordered star appeared in the upper-left corner. The new design was approved in November 1949 and formally adopted in July 1950, partly to distinguish the Ukrainian SSR's flag from that of the Soviet Union at international organisations.

Flag of the Reichskommissariat Ukraine (1941–1944)

The German occupation administration did not establish a distinct Ukrainian national flag. Nazi German flags and military standards were used by the occupying authorities, while Ukrainian national symbols were not recognised as official state symbols. The blue-and-yellow flag continued to have significance among Ukrainian national movements, but it was not the official flag of the Reichskommissariat.

National flag, civil and state ensign, and naval jack of Ukraine (1992–present)

The modern Ukrainian flag is a horizontal bicolour of blue over yellow, officially restored by the Verkhovna Rada on 28 January 1992. The blue is commonly associated with the sky and the yellow with wheat fields. The plain bicolour serves as the national flag and civil and state ensign. Ukraine's naval ensign is a separate white flag bearing a blue cross, with a small national flag in the upper hoist.

Historical note

The blue-and-yellow colours have deep roots in Ukrainian heraldry, particularly the golden lion of Galicia and the banners of western Ukrainian military and cultural organisations. However, Cossack flags were diverse, and the modern national flag should not be treated as a direct, unchanged continuation of any single medieval or Cossack banner. The blue-over-yellow arrangement became established as the national design during the independence movement of 1917–1921 and was restored after the collapse of Soviet rule.

Historical clarification

  • Kyivan Rus had no single, universally recognised national flag. Depictions of a red banner with a golden trident are reconstructions based on princely symbols.
  • The Galicia–Volhynia lion is a heraldic symbol, and the commonly illustrated flag is reconstructed from medieval evidence rather than preserved as a documented national flag.
  • The Cossack Hetmanate and the Zaporizhian Sich were distinct political and military institutions. Their banners varied considerably; the crimson banner with Archangel Michael is particularly associated with the Sich's great standard.
  • The colour order of the 1917–1918 Ukrainian People's Republic flags is historically disputed. Both arrangements appeared, and accounts of the March 1918 adoption have sometimes been interpreted differently. The blue-over-yellow version became the established national design.
  • The Ukrainian SSR flags of 1919, 1929 and 1937 were successive revisions of the red flag and its inscriptions. The 1950 design introduced the blue lower stripe.
  • The Reichskommissariat Ukraine was a German occupation administration, not an independent Ukrainian state with its own national flag.
  • Ukraine's modern national flag was provisionally restored in 1991 and formally adopted on 28 January 1992. The naval ensign is a separate service flag, not simply another name for the national bicolour.
