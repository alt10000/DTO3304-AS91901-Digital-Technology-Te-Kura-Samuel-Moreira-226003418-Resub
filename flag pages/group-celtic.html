Celtic Peoples

About the Celtic group

The Celtic peoples are the Irish, Scots, Welsh, Cornish, Manx and Bretons, who are linked by the survival of Celtic languages and traditions. Only Ireland and Britain appear as countries on this site, so this page looks at the flags that grew out of those lands.

Celtic identity is cultural rather than political, which is why many of its flags belong to regions and not to independent states.

Flags side by side

The Irish tricolour

Thomas Francis Meagher introduced the green, white and orange flag in 1848. Green is linked to the Gaelic and nationalist tradition, orange to the Protestant community, and white to peace between them. It was flown in the Easter Rising of 1916.

Older Irish symbols

Before the tricolour, Ireland was marked by banners of the crown. The Lordship of Ireland used three golden crowns on blue, and the Kingdom of Ireland used a gold harp on blue, an emblem that still appears on the royal arms.

Scotland and the Union

Scotland’s saltire is traditionally traced to a legend of 832, though its familiar form is first confirmed around 1542. It joined with England’s cross in 1606, and in 1801 Saint Patrick’s red saltire was added for Ireland, giving the modern Union Flag.

Other Celtic flags

Wales has its own red dragon flag, Brittany has the black-and-white Gwenn-ha-du designed in the 1920s, and Cornwall flies Saint Piran’s white cross on black. They are not covered on this site but belong to the same wider family of Celtic regional flags.
