Flag of the Second Bulgarian Empire according to Angelino Dalorto (c. 1325)

The Shishman dynasty took the Bulgarian throne in 1323 under Michael Shishman, and the Italian chartmaker Angelino Dalorto marked Bulgaria with a light swallow-tailed banner bearing a red crown-like emblem. It is one of the few visual clues to a medieval Bulgarian flag, but its exact design and colours cannot be confirmed.

Flag of the Second Bulgarian Empire according to Guillem Soler (c. 1380)

Around 1380 the Second Bulgarian Empire was split between Tsar Ivan Shishman at Tarnovo and Ivan Sratsimir at Vidin while the Ottomans advanced. The Majorcan mapmaker Guillem Soler showed Bulgaria with a gold flag bearing a red crown-like emblem, which differs from earlier charts and suggests there was no single standard flag.

Flag of the Tsardom of Vidin (14th century)

Ivan Sratsimir ruled the Tsardom of Vidin from 1356, when it split from the Tarnovo realm, and it survived as western Bulgaria until the Ottomans conquered it in 1396 after the Battle of Nicopolis. The flag is a reconstruction based on heraldry linked to the ruling dynasty, so its exact appearance is uncertain.

Civil and State Flag of the People's Republic of Bulgaria (1947–1948)

Following the abolition of the Bulgarian monarchy in 1946 and the establishment of the People's Republic, a new flag was introduced in 1947. It retained the traditional white, green and red horizontal stripes but added a national emblem in the upper-left corner of the white stripe, featuring a golden lion surrounded by wheat ears beneath a red star and a ribbon bearing the date 9 September 1944.

Civil and State Flag of the People's Republic of Bulgaria (1948–1968)

The emblem was redesigned in 1948 under the new communist constitution, keeping the golden lion, wheat wreath and red star. The lion is Bulgaria’s traditional heraldic animal, the wheat stands for agriculture, and the date 9 September 1944 on the ribbon marks the coup that brought the communists to power.

Civil and State Flag of the People's Republic of Bulgaria (1968–1971)

A revision in 1968 redrew the national emblem’s details while keeping the same symbols on the white stripe. The lion represents Bulgarian statehood, the wheat wreath agriculture, the red star socialism, and the ribbon’s date of 9 September 1944 the communist takeover.

Civil and State Flag of the People's Republic of Bulgaria (1971–1990)

Introduced in 1971, this flag featured a revised national emblem with the dates 681 and 1944 displayed on separate sections of its ribbon. The year 681 commemorated the foundation of the First Bulgarian Empire, while 1944 represented the communist takeover, combining Bulgaria's medieval history with its socialist political identity.

Modern Flag of Bulgaria (1990–present)

Following the fall of communist rule, Bulgaria restored its traditional white, green and red horizontal tricolour on 27 November 1990, removing the communist-era national emblem. The design was subsequently reaffirmed by the 1991 Constitution and standardised by legislation in 1998, representing Bulgarian independence, sovereignty and national identity.
