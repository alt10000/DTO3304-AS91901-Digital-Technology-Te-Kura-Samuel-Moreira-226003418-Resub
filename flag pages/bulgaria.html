Flag of the Second Bulgarian Empire according to Angelino Dalorto (c. 1325)

Depicted on a medieval portolan chart by Italian cartographer Angelino Dalorto, this flag is associated with the Shishman dynasty, which ruled parts of the Second Bulgarian Empire. The historical illustration depicts a light-coloured banner bearing a red heraldic symbol, although its precise design and colours are difficult to establish from the surviving map.

Flag of the Second Bulgarian Empire according to Guillem Soler (c. 1380)

Appearing on a nautical chart produced by Majorcan cartographer Guillem Soler, this flag represents Bulgaria during the later period of the Second Bulgarian Empire. Its depiction differs from earlier Bulgarian heraldic representations, illustrating the variety of symbols used to identify medieval Bulgarian territories and rulers.

Flag of the Tsardom of Vidin (14th century)

Associated with the Tsardom of Vidin, a medieval Bulgarian state that emerged following the fragmentation of the Second Bulgarian Empire, this flag featured heraldic imagery associated with its ruling dynasty. It represented the western Bulgarian territories ruled by Ivan Sratsimir before their conquest by the Ottoman Empire in 1396.

Civil and State Flag of the People's Republic of Bulgaria (1947–1948)

Following the abolition of the Bulgarian monarchy in 1946 and the establishment of the People's Republic, a new flag was introduced in 1947. It retained the traditional white, green and red horizontal stripes but added a national emblem in the upper-left corner of the white stripe, featuring a golden lion surrounded by wheat ears beneath a red star and a ribbon bearing the date 9 September 1944.

Civil and State Flag of the People's Republic of Bulgaria (1948–1968)

In 1948, the Bulgarian national emblem was revised while the flag's white, green and red stripes remained unchanged. The emblem continued to feature a golden lion, wheat wreath and red star, with the date 9 September 1944 retained to commemorate the communist takeover of Bulgaria.

Civil and State Flag of the People's Republic of Bulgaria (1968–1971)

This version retained the established communist-era tricolour but introduced another modification to the national emblem, refining its heraldic details and overall appearance. The red star, golden lion, wheat wreath and commemorative date remained central elements of the design.

Civil and State Flag of the People's Republic of Bulgaria (1971–1990)

Introduced in 1971, this flag featured a revised national emblem with the dates 681 and 1944 displayed on separate sections of its ribbon. The year 681 commemorated the foundation of the First Bulgarian Empire, while 1944 represented the communist takeover, combining Bulgaria's medieval history with its socialist political identity.

Modern Flag of Bulgaria (1990–present)

Following the fall of communist rule, Bulgaria restored its traditional white, green and red horizontal tricolour on 27 November 1990, removing the communist-era national emblem. The design was subsequently reaffirmed by the 1991 Constitution and standardised by legislation in 1998, representing Bulgarian independence, sovereignty and national identity.

Historical note: Bulgaria's modern flag originated in the 19th-century independence movement and was officially established in 1879. During communist rule, the traditional tricolour was retained but modified with increasingly elaborate national emblems. Following the collapse of communism in 1990, the original unadorned tricolour was restored.

One historical clarification: The two medieval flags attributed to Angelino Dalorto and Guillem Soler are based on illustrations in portolan charts, rather than surviving physical flags. Their exact appearance and historical accuracy cannot be definitively established.
