Historical Flags

Flag of the Macedonian Empire (336–323 BC)

There is no reliable historical evidence that Alexander the Great's Macedonian Empire had a single, standardised national flag. Macedonian armies used military standards and symbols, but modern depictions of a distinct Macedonian imperial flag should be treated as reconstructions rather than confirmed historical designs.

Byzantine Ceremonial Labarum (9th–12th centuries)

The Byzantine court used ceremonial and military banners that carried Christian imagery such as the cross and the labarum inherited from Constantine. The double-headed eagle, linked to the Roman imperial tradition, became associated with the empire mainly in its later centuries, and no single flag was standard.

Byzantine Imperial Ensign (c. 1330)

A gold field with a red cross and four firesteels, or Greek letter betas, is linked to the Palaiologos dynasty that ruled Byzantium from 1261 until 1453. A later reading of the four letters is “King of Kings, ruling over kings”. The flag as shown is a reconstruction from medieval charts and later descriptions.

Byzantine Imperial Flag (c. 1400)

By around 1400 the Byzantine Empire had shrunk to Constantinople and parts of the Morea, but its Palaiologan emperors kept the gold-and-red cross banner as their imperial standard. The empire’s long decline ended when Mehmed II captured Constantinople in 1453.

Flag Adopted by the First National Assembly at Epidaurus (1822–1863)

On 13 January 1822, the First National Assembly at Epidaurus adopted a national flag to replace the many different revolutionary banners used during the Greek War of Independence. The land flag consisted of a white cross on a blue field, while the naval version featured nine alternating blue and white stripes with a white cross in a blue canton; the striped design eventually became Greece's familiar national flag.

State and War Flag of the Kingdom of Greece (1863–1924; 1935–1973)

Following the accession of King George I in 1863, a golden royal crown was added to the centre of the white cross on the blue land flag and the striped naval flag. These crowned designs represented the Greek monarchy and were used for official state and military purposes until the monarchy was abolished.

Flag of Greece under the Regime of the Colonels (1970–1975)

During the military dictatorship, the striped blue-and-white flag became the sole national flag in 1969, and its proportions were changed in 1970. This version used a particularly dark shade of blue and a 7:12 aspect ratio; it remained in use until the restoration of democratic government and the return to the earlier flag arrangements in 1975.

Flag of Greece (1978–present)

The current Greek flag consists of nine alternating blue and white horizontal stripes, with a white cross on a blue square in the upper-left corner. Officially established as the sole national flag in 1978, it combines the cross representing Greek Orthodox Christianity with the traditional blue and white colours associated with Greece.
