Historical Flags

Battle standard – hypothetical reconstruction according to Casali (1465)

This proposed reconstruction depicts an early Sammarinese military standard based on historical interpretations of the Republic's heraldic colours and symbols. However, its precise appearance is not confirmed by surviving contemporary evidence. It should therefore be treated as a speculative reconstruction, rather than a securely documented national or military flag.

Flag hoisted at the Guaita (1829)

This design represents the earliest recorded use of the white-and-azure (light-blue) horizontal bicolour associated with San Marino. The flag is traditionally connected with the Guaita, one of the republic's three historic towers. Although the precise date when the bicolour was first adopted remains unknown, its colours were already established by the early nineteenth century.

Typical unstandardised flag (early 19th century–2011)

The flag consisted of two horizontal stripes, white above light blue, usually displaying the national coat of arms in the centre. The coat of arms depicted three towers on three mountains, surrounded by oak and laurel branches and topped by a crown, with the motto LIBERTAS (Liberty) beneath. Before 2011, the precise proportions, colours and heraldic details were not consistently regulated, resulting in numerous variations.

National flag of San Marino (2011–present)

On 22 July 2011, Constitutional Law No. 1 formally standardised the national flag and coat of arms. It consists of two equal horizontal bands of white and light blue, with the official national coat of arms in the centre. The coat of arms features a crowned shield containing three silver towers on three green mountains, framed by oak and laurel branches, with the motto LIBERTAS displayed on a scroll below. The official proportions are 3:4, although a 2:3 version is permitted for international events and sporting competitions.

Historical note

San Marino's white-and-azure colours are associated with liberty and peace. Although the exact adoption date of the bicolour is unknown, a white-and-blue cockade was officially commissioned in 1797, and the first recorded reference to a flag in these colours dates to 1829. The national coat of arms was standardised in 1862, while the flag's precise design was finally regulated in 2011.

Historical clarification

  • The 1465 battle standard is hypothetical. An archival document dated 4 September 1465 records the commissioning of a flag with three vertical bands: yellow and crimson at the sides, with white in the centre. The Casali reconstruction should not be confused with this documented description.
  • The white-and-azure bicolour predates its formal standardisation. Its exact introduction date is unknown, although its use is documented by 1829.
  • The pre-2011 flag had no universally fixed design. The coat of arms, shade of blue and proportions varied between examples.
  • The 2011 law standardised the existing flag rather than introducing an entirely new design. The modern flag therefore preserves the traditional white-and-light-blue colours and central coat of arms.
