Nordic Peoples

About the Nordic group

The Nordic countries share a strong cultural identity and a flag pattern that appears nowhere else in the same form: the Nordic cross, an off-centre cross that stretches to the edges of the flag.

Denmark, Sweden, Norway and Iceland are Scandinavian or North Germanic, while Finland has Finnish roots but belongs to the same family of flags.

Flags side by side

The Dannebrog as the model

Denmark’s flag is among the oldest in continuous use, and legend says it fell from the sky at the Battle of Lyndanisse in 1219. Its design was copied by its neighbours: every Nordic flag has a cross whose upright arm sits closer to the hoist.

Colours and meanings

Each country gave the shared pattern its own meaning. Sweden’s blue and yellow come from its national arms, Norway’s red, white and blue echo the colours of liberty, Iceland’s are traditionally read as fire, ice and mountains, and Finland’s as lakes and snow.

Unions and their flags

Several Nordic countries were once joined under a single crown. The Kalmar Union flag combined Denmark, Norway and Sweden in the late Middle Ages, and the later union between Sweden and Norway used a quartered mark known as the herring salad.

Flags that never were

Iceland’s first flag proposal came in 1809 from the adventurer Jørgen Jørgensen, showing three white stockfish on blue. Finland’s first state flag in 1918 carried the lion of the national arms in the cross, reflecting the possibility that the country might become a monarchy.
