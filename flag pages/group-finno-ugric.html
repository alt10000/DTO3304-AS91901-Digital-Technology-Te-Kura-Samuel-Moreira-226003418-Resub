Finno-Ugric Peoples

About the Finno-Ugric group

The Finno-Ugric peoples speak languages of the Uralic family. In this study they include the Finns, the Estonians and the Hungarians, along with smaller groups elsewhere in northern Europe and Russia.

Their flags are a useful contrast to the other groups, because language kinship did not produce any shared colours or symbols.

Flags side by side

Three different traditions

Finland’s flag belongs to the Nordic cross family, Estonia’s comes from a student society, and Hungary’s from the arms of its medieval kings. Each flag follows the history of its own state rather than the language its people speak.

Hungarian heraldry

Hungary’s tricolour draws its red and white from the Árpád stripes, the banner of its first dynasty, and its green from the hills in the arms. This heraldic link to the kingdom’s founders makes the flag very different from the Finnish and Estonian designs.

Flags without states

Many Finno-Ugric peoples have no country of their own. The Sámi of northern Scandinavia adopted their own flag in 1986, using a blue-green-red-yellow design with a circle, and similar regional flags exist for other groups. Like the Celtic peoples, they show how culture can have a flag without a state.
