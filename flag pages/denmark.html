Historical Flags

Flag of the North Sea Empire (1016–1035)

The North Sea Empire, ruled by Cnut the Great, is traditionally associated with the Raven Banner, a black raven emblem on a coloured field. This banner was used by several Viking rulers and Scandinavian forces, although its exact design and colours during Cnut's reign are not conclusively established.

Flag of the Kalmar Union (1397–1523)

The Kalmar Union, which united Denmark, Norway and Sweden under a common monarch, is associated with a red Scandinavian cross on a yellow field. This design is based on two letters from King Eric of Pomerania in 1430 referring to a "banner of the realms"; no surviving original flag or contemporary illustration confirms its appearance, so modern depictions are reconstructions.

Civil Flag and Ensign of Denmark (1748–present)

The Dannebrog consists of a white Scandinavian cross on a red field, with the vertical arm shifted towards the hoist. Officially established for merchant shipping in 1748, it became Denmark's national flag and is one of the world's oldest continuously used national flag designs.

Historical note: The legend claims that the Dannebrog fell from heaven during the Battle of Lyndanisse in Estonia on 15 June 1219, helping the Danish forces win. However, the earliest known surviving depiction dates from the late 14th century, and its historical origins remain uncertain.
