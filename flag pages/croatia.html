Historical Flags

Flag of Croatian Troops (1526–1527)

A military banner associated with Croatian forces around the Battle of Mohács. It is commonly illustrated with a red-and-white checkerboard pattern, though the precise design and historical attribution are not certain.

Flag of the Socialist Republic of Croatia (1947–1990)

Adopted on 18 January 1947, this flag featured three horizontal stripes of red, white and blue, with a red five-pointed star outlined in gold at the centre. It represented Croatia as one of the constituent republics of socialist Yugoslavia, with the star symbolising communism and the Partisan movement's victory during the Second World War.

Flag of Croatia (1990–present)

Following the collapse of communist rule, Croatia restored its traditional red, white and blue tricolour, replacing the communist star with the historic red-and-white chequered coat of arms. The modern design, officially adopted on 21 December 1990, also features a crown of five smaller regional shields representing Croatia's historical regions, symbolising national heritage, unity and independence.

Historical note: The Croatian red, white and blue tricolour dates back to 1848 and has remained the foundation of the country's national flag through several political transformations. The most significant changes involved replacing the central emblem: first with a communist star during Yugoslav rule and later with the historic chequered coat of arms following the restoration of Croatian democracy.
