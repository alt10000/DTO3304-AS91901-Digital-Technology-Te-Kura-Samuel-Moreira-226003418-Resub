Historical Flags

Proposed Flag of Iceland under Jørgen Jørgensen (1809)

During his brief rule over Iceland in the summer of 1809, Danish adventurer Jørgen Jørgensen proposed a blue flag bearing three white stockfish (dried cod). It is considered the first known proposal for a distinct Icelandic national flag, although no original example survives and its exact arrangement is uncertain.

National Flag and Civil Ensign of Iceland (1918–1944)

Following Iceland's recognition as a separate kingdom in personal union with Denmark on 1 December 1918, the blue flag with a white Nordic cross and a red cross inside it became the official national flag. Its blue was originally specified as a lighter ultramarine shade, distinguishing it from the darker blue used in the modern design.

Civil Flag and Ensign of Iceland (1944–present)

Following the establishment of the Republic of Iceland on 17 June 1944, the national flag retained its blue field, white Nordic cross and red inner cross, but adopted a darker blue shade. The Nordic cross reflects Iceland's historical and cultural connections with the other Scandinavian countries.

Historical note: Before developing its own national flag, Iceland generally used the Danish flag because it was under Danish rule. The modern Icelandic flag developed from earlier proposals for a blue-and-white Nordic cross, with the red cross added in 1915 to distinguish it from similar flags.
