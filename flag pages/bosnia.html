Bosnia and Herzegovina

Overview

A crossroads of Catholic, Orthodox and Muslim cultures, shaped by medieval kingdoms, Ottoman rule and Austro-Hungarian administration.

Historical Flags

Flag of the Bosnian Banate (c. 1354)

Associated with the medieval Banate of Bosnia under Ban Stephen II Kotromanić, this banner featured a blue field with a white cross pattée in the centre and a swallow-tailed design. It is among the earliest recorded heraldic symbols associated with the medieval Bosnian state.

Royal Flag of Tvrtko I of Bosnia (1377–1391)

Used during the reign of King Tvrtko I, this royal banner featured a white field bearing the coat of arms of the Kotromanić dynasty, consisting of a blue shield with a white diagonal stripe and six golden fleurs-de-lis. The design represented the establishment of the Kingdom of Bosnia and became an important symbol of medieval Bosnian royalty.

Flag of Herzegovina (1448–1482)

Associated with the Duchy of Saint Sava under Duke Stjepan Vukčić Kosača, this flag featured a red field with two white cross pattées positioned towards the hoist and a three-pointed fringe at the fly. It represented the medieval Duchy of Saint Sava, from which the historical name Herzegovina derives.

Banner used for Rama at Ferdinand II's Coronation (1618)

This swallow-tailed banner represented the historical region of Rama during the coronation of Ferdinand II as King of Hungary. It featured a blue field with a central heraldic shield, reflecting the region's historical identity and its association with the medieval Bosnian crown.

Flag of the Bosnian Uprising (1831–1832)

Used during the rebellion led by Husein-kapetan Gradaščević against Ottoman authority, this flag featured a green field with a yellow crescent and star. It became a symbol of the Bosnian movement for greater autonomy within the Ottoman Empire, although the precise date of its adoption is uncertain.

Flag of the Austro-Hungarian-Occupied Province of Bosnia (1878–1908)

Introduced following the Austro-Hungarian occupation of Bosnia in 1878, this flag featured two horizontal stripes of red and yellow, with a central coat of arms depicting an arm emerging from a cloud holding a sword. The coat of arms was inspired by the heraldry of medieval Bosnian nobleman Stjepan Vukčić Kosača and was intended to establish a distinct provincial identity under Habsburg administration.

Flag of the Condominium of Bosnia and Herzegovina (1908–1918)

Following the formal annexation of Bosnia and Herzegovina by Austria-Hungary in 1908, the provincial flag was simplified to a horizontal red-and-yellow bicolour without the central coat of arms. The design remained in use until the collapse of Austria-Hungary at the end of the First World War in 1918.

Flag of the Bosnian-Herzegovinian Partisans (1940–1946)

Used by the Bosnian-Herzegovinian Partisans during the Second World War, this flag featured a red field with a central red five-pointed star outlined in yellow. The design represented the communist-led resistance movement against Axis occupation and its collaborators, as well as the wider Yugoslav Partisan struggle for liberation.

Flag of the Socialist Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina (1946–1992)

Adopted on 31 December 1946, this flag featured a red field with the Yugoslav national flag in the upper-left corner. The red background represented the socialist and national liberation movements, while the Yugoslav tricolour symbolised Bosnia and Herzegovina's position as one of the constituent republics of socialist Yugoslavia.

Flag of the Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina (1992–1998)

Adopted following Bosnia and Herzegovina's declaration of independence in 1992, this flag featured a white field with a blue shield in the centre, bearing a white diagonal stripe and six golden fleurs-de-lis. The design was based on the medieval coat of arms of the Kotromanić dynasty and symbolised the country's historical heritage and newly restored independence.

Modern Flag of Bosnia and Herzegovina (1998–present)

Introduced on 4 February 1998 by the international High Representative after the country's political representatives failed to agree on a new national flag, this design features a blue field, a yellow triangle and a diagonal row of white stars. The triangle represents the country's geographical shape and its three constituent peoples, while the stars symbolise Europe and Bosnia and Herzegovina's aspirations towards European integration.

Historical note: Bosnia and Herzegovina has had a particularly diverse flag history, with symbols ranging from medieval royal heraldry and Ottoman-era imagery to Austro-Hungarian provincial flags and socialist Yugoslav designs. Its modern flag is notably different from its predecessors, having been deliberately designed to avoid favouring any particular ethnic or national group.
