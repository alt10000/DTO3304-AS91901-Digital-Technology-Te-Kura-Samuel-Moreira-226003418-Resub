Flag of the Duchy of Limburg (1214–1242)

This historical banner is depicted as a white field with a narrow green vertical stripe along the hoist and a red, crowned lion rampant with golden claws and tongue. The lion was the principal heraldic emblem of the Duchy of Limburg. The duchy later came under Luxembourgish control through inheritance, before passing into Burgundian possession.

Flag of the County and Duchy of Luxembourg (1242–1443)

The banner featured ten horizontal alternating blue and white stripes, with a red crowned lion rampant bearing golden claws and tongue superimposed in the centre. This design was derived from the arms of the Counts of Luxembourg and became the basis for the heraldic colours of the country. The lion is traditionally associated with the House of Luxembourg.

Flag of the Duchy of Burgundy under Philip the Good (1443–1556)

After Philip the Good acquired Luxembourg in 1443, the duchy became part of the Burgundian territories. The Burgundian banner is generally represented by a white field bearing a red diagonal saltire, often depicted with irregular, knotted branches. This became known as the Cross of Burgundy and was associated with the House of Valois-Burgundy and its territories. Its use specifically within Luxembourg is less clearly documented than its association with the wider Burgundian realm.

Flag of Luxembourg (1848 and 1944)

The familiar horizontal red-white-light-blue tricolour emerged during the Belgian Revolution of 1830, when Luxembourg's inhabitants displayed the colours associated with their heraldic tradition. Its arrangement was formally prescribed in 1845 and became established as the national flag around 1848. The same flag returned to public use following Luxembourg's liberation from German occupation in 1944.

National flag of Luxembourg (1972–present; colours and specifications updated in 1993)

The national flag consists of three equal horizontal stripes of red, white and light blue. Although the design had been used since the nineteenth century, the Law on National Emblems of 23 June 1972 provided a legal framework for Luxembourg's national symbols. A Grand-Ducal regulation of 27 July 1993 specified the flag's colours, including the distinctive sky blue (Pantone 299C), which differentiates it from the darker blue of the Netherlands.

Historical note: Luxembourg's national colours originate in the heraldry of the medieval County and Duchy of Luxembourg. The red lion and blue-and-white stripes of the historic coat of arms provided the colours for the modern red-white-blue tricolour. The country's civil ensign, known as the Roude Léiw (Red Lion), preserves the medieval heraldic imagery through ten alternating blue and white stripes with a red crowned lion.

Historical clarification: The medieval banners listed here are heraldic reconstructions rather than necessarily standardised national flags in the modern sense. The Burgundian Cross was a wider dynastic and military banner, and its precise use in Luxembourg during Philip the Good's reign is not firmly established. Also, the 1972 date refers to the legal framework for national emblems, while the precise colour specifications were established in 1993.
