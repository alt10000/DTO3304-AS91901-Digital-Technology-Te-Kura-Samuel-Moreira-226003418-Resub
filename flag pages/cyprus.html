Flag of the Greek War of Independence (1821–1827)

Used by the Cypriot volunteers who fought alongside Greek forces during the Greek War of Independence. It was a white banner bearing a large blue cross, with the Greek inscription «ΕΛΛΗΝΙΚΗ ΣΗΜΑΙΑ – ΠΑΤΡΙΣ ΚΥΠΡΟΣ» (“Greek Flag – Homeland Cyprus”) in the upper-left corner.

First Flag of Cyprus (1960)

This was an early proposal for the newly independent republic, showing the island as a plain outline above two olive branches on white. It expressed the same idea as the final flag, peace between the island’s communities, but was replaced by the copper-coloured design later in 1960.

Second Flag of Cyprus (1960–2006)

The original national flag adopted upon independence on 16 August 1960, designed by Turkish Cypriot artist İsmet Güney. It depicted the island in copper-orange above two green olive branches, symbolising the island's copper resources and the hope for peace between the Greek and Turkish Cypriot communities.

National Flag of Cyprus (2006–present)

A 2006 revision refined the colours and the olive branches and fixed the proportions at 2:3, without changing the 1960 design’s meaning. The copper-coloured island recalls the metal the island is traditionally said to be named for, while the white field and green branches stand for peace between Greek and Turkish Cypriots.

Historical note: Cyprus's national flag was deliberately designed to avoid the colours blue and red, as well as the cross and crescent, so that it would not favour either the Greek or Turkish communities.
