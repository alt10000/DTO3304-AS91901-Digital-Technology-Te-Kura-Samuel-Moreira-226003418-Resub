Baltic States

About the Baltic group

Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania are neighbours on the Baltic Sea who share a history of foreign rule, brief independence between the world wars, Soviet occupation and the restoration of independence around 1990. Culturally, Latvians and Lithuanians are Baltic peoples, while Estonians are Finnic.

Their flags are three tricolours that were banned under Soviet rule and raised again as symbols of freedom.

Flags side by side

Three tricolours, three stories

Estonia’s colours come from a student society flag consecrated on 4 June 1884. Lithuania’s were chosen by a commission in 1918, and Latvia’s red-white-red is traditionally traced to a banner described in a medieval chronicle.

Under Soviet rule

After 1940 the national flags were replaced with red Soviet flags. From 1953 each carried a hammer, sickle and star in the corner and a stripe along the bottom: wavy blue and white for the sea in Estonia and Latvia, and white above green in Lithuania.

Raised again

Lithuania restored its tricolour in 1988, and Latvia and Estonia followed in 1990. In 1989 about two million people joined hands across all three countries in the Baltic Way, with the banned flags among the symbols of protest. Full independence was recognised in 1991.

An older symbol

Alongside its modern flag, Lithuania carries the Vytis, a mounted knight on a red field, which was the banner of the medieval grand dukes. It shows that the three flags have different roots even though the countries share a story.
