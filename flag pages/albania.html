Albania

Overview

Ruled in turn by Rome, Byzantium and the Ottomans, Albania declared independence in 1912. Its double-headed eagle recalls the medieval hero Skanderbeg.

Historical Flags

Flag of the Bajrak of Kashnjeti (1878)

Unfurled by Ded Kol Bajraktari on 10 June 1878 during the League of Prizren, this flag represented the Bajrak of Kashnjeti. It features a black double-headed eagle on a red background, a silver sun above it, and the inscription MIRDITA below, symbolising Albanian resistance and regional identity.

Flag of the Provisional Government (1912–1914)

Used following Albania's declaration of independence on 28 November 1912, this flag featured a black double-headed eagle on a red background. It became the principal symbol of the newly established Albanian state under Ismail Qemali's government.

Flag of the self-proclaimed Republic of Central Albania (1913–1914)

Associated with Essad Pasha Toptani's breakaway government, this flag featured a light-red field with a white star-shaped polygon in the lower-right corner. Its design resembled the Ottoman flag, although the crescent had been removed.

Flag of the Principality of Albania (1914–1920)

Used during the reign of Prince Wilhelm of Wied, this flag retained the traditional red background and black double-headed eagle. The eagle featured a golden shield bearing a blue peacock on its chest, combining Albanian national symbolism with elements of the Wied royal family.

Flag of the Autonomous Province of Korçë (1916–1920)

Established under the French administration of Korçë, this flag featured the traditional standard of Skanderbeg, decorated with a French tricolour ribbon. Its design represented the province's Albanian identity while acknowledging its special relationship with France.

Flag used at the Congress of Lushnjë (1920)

This flag was used during the Congress of Lushnjë, which played a crucial role in restoring Albanian independence and establishing a new national government. Made of dark-red silk, it featured a black double-headed eagle similar to the one used by the Provisional Government.

Flag of the Albanian Republic (1920–1926)

Following the establishment of the republican government in 1920, Albania continued using the traditional red flag with a black double-headed eagle. The design represented national sovereignty and the country's continued independence following the First World War.

Flag of the Albanian Republic (1926–1928)

Used during the later years of the Albanian Republic under Ahmet Zogu, this flag maintained the red background and black double-headed eagle. It represented the republic until Zogu's proclamation as King Zog I in September 1928.

Flag of the Albanian Kingdom (1928–1939)

Following Ahmet Zogu's coronation as King Zog I, Albania retained its traditional red flag and black double-headed eagle. The monarchy introduced a new state emblem featuring Skanderbeg's helmet, which became associated with the royal period.

Flag of the Kingdom of Albania (1939–1943)

Following the Italian invasion in April 1939, Albania became an Italian-controlled kingdom under King Victor Emmanuel III. The flag incorporated the black double-headed eagle alongside the Italian Fascist fasces, Skanderbeg's helmet and elements of the House of Savoy.

Flag used during the German occupation of Albania (1943–1944)

Following Italy's surrender in 1943, German forces occupied Albania and the country reverted to the flag regulations of the pre-Italian monarchy. Various versions of the traditional red flag with the black double-headed eagle were used during this period.

Flag of the Democratic Government of Albania (1944–1946)

Used by the communist-led Democratic Government following the liberation of Albania, this flag featured a black double-headed eagle on a red background. It represented the new post-war government and the beginning of Albania's transition towards a communist state.

Flag of the People's Socialist Republic of Albania (1946–1992)

Following the establishment of the People's Republic in 1946, a golden-outlined red five-pointed star was added above the black double-headed eagle. The star represented communism and the country's socialist political system, remaining part of the flag until the fall of communist rule in 1992.

Flag of Albania (1992–2002)

Following the collapse of communism in 1992, the red five-pointed star was removed, restoring the traditional red flag with the black double-headed eagle. This change symbolised Albania's transition away from communist rule and towards democracy.

Flag of the Republic of Albania (2002–present)

Standardised through legislation on 22 July 2002, the current Albanian flag features a blood-red field with a black double-headed eagle at its centre. It preserves the historic national symbol associated with Skanderbeg and represents Albanian independence, identity and sovereignty.

A small historical clarification: Entries 7 and 8 are two different historical flag entries, but their descriptions are very similar. Both use the traditional red field and black double-headed eagle, rather than representing two radically different flag designs.
