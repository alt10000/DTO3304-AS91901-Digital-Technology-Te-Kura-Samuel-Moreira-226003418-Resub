Monaco

Historical Flags

Flag of Monaco (1814–1881)

Following the end of French rule in 1814, the Principality of Monaco restored its traditional princely banner. It featured a white field bearing the coat of arms of the House of Grimaldi, which included a shield decorated with red and white lozenges (diamond shapes). This design represented the ruling dynasty and the restored sovereignty of the principality.

National flag of Monaco (1881–present)

On 4 April 1881, Prince Charles III officially adopted the modern Monegasque national flag. It consists of two equal horizontal stripes, red above white. These colours have been associated with the House of Grimaldi since at least 1339 and are derived from its traditional heraldic colours. The flag serves as Monaco's national flag and civil ensign.

Historical note: Monaco's red and white colours originate from the heraldry of the House of Grimaldi. The red-and-white lozenges remain part of the princely coat of arms, while the modern national flag uses a simplified bicolour without an emblem.

Historical clarification: The 1814 flag is generally depicted as the princely coat of arms on a white field, rather than the modern red-over-white bicolour. Monaco's current national flag was formally established in 1881. The Principality also has a separate princely standard, consisting of the Grimaldi coat of arms on a white background; this should not be confused with its national flag.
