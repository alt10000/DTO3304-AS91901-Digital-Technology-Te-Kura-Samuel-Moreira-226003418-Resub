Finland

Historical Flags

State Flag and Ensign of Finland (1918–1920)

Adopted on 29 May 1918, this design featured a blue Nordic cross on a white field, with Finland's coat of arms displayed at the centre of the cross. The coat of arms included the crowned Finnish lion, reflecting the possibility that Finland might become a monarchy; the military ensign used a swallow-tailed version of the design.

State Flag and Ensign of Finland (1920–1978)

Following Finland's establishment as a republic, the crown was removed from the Finnish lion on 12 February 1920, and the coat of arms was adjusted to fit the centre of the cross. The state flag retained the coat of arms, while the civil flag and ensign used the plain blue-cross design; the military ensign retained its swallow-tailed shape.

Flag of Finland (1978–present)

The modern Finnish flag was established under the Flag Act of 1978, which came into force on 1 June. It consists of a blue Nordic cross on a white background, with the blue traditionally representing Finland's lakes and the white representing its winter snow. The state flag retains the Finnish coat of arms in the centre, while the national flag used by the public has no coat of arms.

Historical note: Finland's blue-and-white cross design was officially adopted shortly after independence from Russia in December 1917. Its Nordic cross reflects Finland's historical and cultural connections with the other Nordic countries.
