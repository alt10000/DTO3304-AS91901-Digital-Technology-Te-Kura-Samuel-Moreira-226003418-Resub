Overview

Historical Flags

Flag of the Duchy of Courland and Semigallia (1561/1562–1795)

The duchy's state flag was a horizontal bicolour of two equal stripes, red above white. Its civil ensign used a more elaborate design, with a red-and-white chequered section at the hoist and horizontal red-and-white stripes on the fly. The duchy existed as a vassal state of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth until it was annexed by the Russian Empire in 1795.

Flag of the Latvian Socialist Soviet Republic (1919–1920)

The short-lived Soviet government in Latvia used a red flag bearing the gold abbreviation LSPR (Latvijas Sociālistiskā Padomju Republika) in the upper-left corner. The flag represented the Soviet republic established during the Latvian War of Independence, which was defeated by Latvian and allied forces in 1920.

Flag of the Latvian Soviet Socialist Republic (1940–1941; 1944–1953)

Following the Soviet occupation and annexation of Latvia in 1940, the republic adopted a red flag featuring a gold hammer and sickle, with the gold abbreviation LPSR (Latvijas Padomju Sociālistiskā Republika) above them. It was used during the first Soviet occupation and again after Soviet forces reoccupied Latvia in 1944.

Flag of the Latvian Soviet Socialist Republic (1953–1990)

Adopted on 17 January 1953, this design retained the red Soviet field and added a gold hammer and sickle with a red star outlined in gold in the upper-left corner. Along the lower edge were alternating blue and white wavy stripes, representing Latvia's waterways and maritime character. The reverse side omitted the Soviet symbols.

State flag and civil ensign of Latvia (1990–present)

Latvia restored its traditional national flag on 27 February 1990, before regaining full independence in 1991. It consists of a dark carmine-red field with a narrow white horizontal stripe through the centre; the red-white-red stripes have a 2:1:2 proportion, and the flag itself has a 1:2 width-to-length ratio. The design serves as Latvia's national and state flag and as its civil ensign.

Historical note: Latvia's red-white-red flag has much older origins than the modern republic. It is traditionally associated with a description in the Livonian Rhymed Chronicle of a red banner with a white stripe, carried by Latvian warriors near Cēsis in 1279. The modern design was popularised in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, and was officially recognised in 1921.

Historical clarification: The 1919–1920 Latvian Socialist Soviet Republic and the Soviet Latvian republic established in 1940 were separate political entities. Their flags also differed: the earlier flag bore the abbreviation LSPR, while the later Soviet flag used LPSR and Soviet imagery.
