Ireland

Overview

Historical Flags

Heraldic Banner of the Lordship of Ireland (1386–1542)

This banner is traditionally depicted with three golden crowns arranged vertically on a blue field, surrounded by a white border. The design was based on the heraldic arms associated with the Lordship of Ireland and is recorded in connection with Richard II's grant of the title Duke of Ireland to Robert de Vere in 1386. However, its use as an actual historical flag is uncertain, and it is better understood as a heraldic reconstruction.

Standard of the Kingdom of Ireland (1542–1801)

The royal standard featured a golden harp with silver strings on a blue field, representing Ireland and its royal identity under the English and later British monarchy. The harp became an established Irish heraldic emblem during the reign of Henry VIII and was incorporated into the British Royal Standard after the 1801 Act of Union.

Flag of Ireland (1937–present)

The Irish tricolour consists of three vertical stripes of green, white and orange. First introduced by Irish nationalist Thomas Francis Meagher in 1848, it became an important symbol of Irish nationalism and was flown during the Easter Rising of 1916. It was officially adopted by the Irish Free State in 1922 and given constitutional recognition in 1937. The green represents the Gaelic and nationalist tradition, orange represents the Protestant community associated with William of Orange, and white symbolises the hope for peace and unity between them.

Historical note: Before the tricolour became the principal national flag, Ireland was commonly represented by a golden harp. A golden harp on a green field became a popular nationalist symbol from the 17th century onwards, while the older royal heraldic harp appeared on a blue field. The tricolour's three colours came to symbolise the aspiration for unity between Ireland's different traditions.
