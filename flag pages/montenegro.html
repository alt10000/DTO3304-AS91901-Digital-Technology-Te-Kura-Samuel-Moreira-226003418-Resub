Historical Flags

Flag of the medieval Montenegrin state of Zeta (1451–1496)

The banner associated with the Crnojević dynasty is depicted as a red field bearing a golden double-headed eagle. The eagle was derived from the dynasty's heraldic arms and became an important symbol of Montenegrin statehood. The design is reconstructed from historical heraldic evidence, including a description of the Crnojević family's coat of arms granted by the Venetian authorities in 1473.

Flag of the Prince-Bishopric of Montenegro (1516–1852)

The traditional military banner, known as the krstaš-barjak (cross banner), featured a cross on a red field, sometimes depicted with a yellow border. It symbolised the Orthodox Christian identity of Montenegro and was used by its rulers and military forces during the period of theocratic government. An 1838 description records a white flag with a small red cross in the centre, illustrating that variations existed.

Flag of the Principality of Montenegro under Danilo I (1852–1860)

When Danilo I transformed Montenegro from a theocratic state into a secular principality in 1852, he initially retained the traditional cross banner, adding his golden initials, Д. I., to its centre. In 1858, a new military banner was introduced: a red field bearing a white double-headed eagle, the ruler's initials and a golden lion beneath the eagle. This more elaborate design became an important model for later Montenegrin state and military flags.

Flag of the Principality of Montenegro under Nicholas I (1860–1905)

During Nicholas I's reign, the red-blue-white tricolour became increasingly prominent. First displayed under Danilo in 1860, it was inspired by wider Pan-Slavic and European revolutionary flag traditions. Nicholas also continued using red military and princely banners featuring the white double-headed eagle, with his own initials replacing those of Danilo. The tricolour gradually became Montenegro's recognised national colours, while the cross banners remained associated with military use.

State flag of the Principality (1905–1910) and Kingdom of Montenegro (1910–1918)

The state flag combined three horizontal stripes of red, blue and white with a white double-headed eagle in the centre. The eagle carried the traditional royal insignia, including a crown, sceptre and orb, while a shield bearing a golden lion appeared on its breast. The 1905 Constitution formally identified red, bluish and white as the national colours. When Nicholas I proclaimed Montenegro a kingdom in 1910, the flag's basic design remained unchanged.

Flag of the People's/Socialist Republic of Montenegro (1946–1992/1993)

Following the Second World War and the establishment of Montenegro as a constituent republic of socialist Yugoslavia, the traditional red-blue-white tricolour was restored with a red five-pointed star outlined in gold at its centre. It was adopted on 31 December 1946 and remained in use through the People's Republic and subsequent Socialist Republic periods.

Flag of the Republic of Montenegro (1993–2004)

After the breakup of socialist Yugoslavia, Montenegro adopted a revised version of its traditional tricolour. The red five-pointed star was removed, and the central blue stripe was changed to a lighter, bluish shade. The flag had three equal horizontal stripes of red, light blue and white and was used while Montenegro remained part of the Federal Republic of Yugoslavia.

National flag of Montenegro (2004–present)

Adopted on 13 July 2004, the current flag consists of a red field surrounded by a golden border, with the national coat of arms in the centre. The coat of arms depicts a golden double-headed eagle wearing a crown and holding a sceptre and orb, with a shield bearing a golden lion passant on a green field. The design draws on the heraldic traditions of the Crnojević and Petrović-Njegoš dynasties.

Historical note: Montenegro's flag history is closely connected to the double-headed eagle, the cross banner and the red-blue-white tricolour. The double-headed eagle became a central symbol of Montenegrin rulers and statehood, while the cross banner represented the country's Orthodox Christian and military traditions. The tricolour emerged during the nineteenth century and was formally recognised through the 1905 Constitution.

Historical clarification: The medieval Zeta banner and some early Montenegrin flags are historical reconstructions rather than fully documented national flags in the modern sense. The flag under Nicholas I also requires a distinction between the tricolour used as the national colours and the eagle-bearing military and dynastic standards. The 1905 Constitution specified the national colours but did not precisely prescribe a single flag design with a coat of arms; surviving evidence shows that red banners and tricolours bearing the coat of arms were both used for official purposes.
