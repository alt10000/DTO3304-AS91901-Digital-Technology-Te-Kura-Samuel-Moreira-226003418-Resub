Historical Flags

Flag of the Burgundian Netherlands (1384–1482)

The banner associated with the Burgundian Netherlands featured a white field bearing the red Cross of Burgundy, a diagonal saltire formed from two crossed, roughly trimmed branches. The design was associated with the Dukes of Burgundy and was accompanied in some depictions by the duke's firesteel emblem and symbols of the Order of the Golden Fleece. It represented the growing political unity of the Burgundian territories in the Low Countries.

Flag of the Habsburg Netherlands (1482–1556)

Following the death of Mary of Burgundy in 1482, the Netherlands passed into the House of Habsburg through her marriage to Maximilian I. The Cross of Burgundy continued to be used on a white field as a common dynastic and military emblem across the Habsburg territories. It symbolised the Burgundian inheritance of the Habsburg rulers and remained prominent in their military standards.

Flag of the Spanish Netherlands (1556–1714)

Under Spanish Habsburg rule, the Cross of Burgundy remained an important emblem. It was commonly displayed as a red diagonal saltire on a white field, particularly on military and imperial banners. A separate design associated with the Spanish Netherlands featured three horizontal stripes of red, white and yellow, with the red Cross of Burgundy across the central white stripe. These designs reflected the territory's connection to both the Burgundian inheritance and the Spanish monarchy.

The Prince's Flag (1572–1795)

The Prince's Flag (Prinsenvlag) was a horizontal tricolour of orange, white and blue, inspired by the heraldic colours of William I, Prince of Orange. It emerged during the Dutch Revolt against Spanish rule and was first documented in 1572. The flag became a symbol of the Dutch struggle for independence and was used by the rebellious provinces and their naval forces. During the seventeenth century, the orange stripe was gradually replaced by red, although the orange version continued to be flown.

Flag of the Kingdom of the Netherlands (1815–present)

Following the establishment of the Kingdom of the Netherlands in 1815, the red-white-blue horizontal tricolour became the country's national flag. It evolved from the earlier Dutch red-white-blue flag, which had gradually replaced the orange-white-blue Prince's Flag during the seventeenth century. The colours were formally established by royal decree in 1937, specifying bright vermilion red, white and cobalt blue. The flag continues to represent the Kingdom of the Netherlands today.

Historical note: The Netherlands' flag history reflects its transition from Burgundian and Habsburg dynastic rule to independence and monarchy. The Cross of Burgundy represented the earlier ruling dynasties, while the orange-white-blue Prince's Flag became a symbol of resistance against Spanish rule. Its gradual transformation into the red-white-blue tricolour produced one of the world's oldest continuously used national flag designs.

Historical clarification: The Cross of Burgundy was primarily a dynastic and military emblem, rather than a standardised national flag in the modern sense. The Spanish Netherlands also used several different military and regional banners, so the red-white-yellow tricolour should be understood as one associated design rather than the only flag used throughout the territory. Similarly, the Prince's Flag did not disappear immediately when red replaced orange; both versions continued to be used.
