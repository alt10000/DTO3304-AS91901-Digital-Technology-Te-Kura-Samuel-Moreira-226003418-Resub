Serbia

Historical Flags

Serbian flag history spans medieval dynastic banners, the red-blue-white tricolour adopted in the nineteenth century, and the modern state flag with the Serbian coat of arms. The medieval examples require particular caution: surviving descriptions and depictions are limited, and some familiar designs are later reconstructions rather than securely documented national flags.

The nineteenth-century flags are especially easy to confuse because the Serbian tricolour was formally arranged red-blue-white from 1839 onward, but the short-lived 1835 design used a different order. The four stars added in 1839 represented Ottoman suzerainty and were removed after Serbia gained international recognition of its independence in 1878.

Flag of Serbia (recorded in 1281)

The earliest known written reference to a Serbian flag appears in an inventory of King Stefan Vladislav's treasury, which had been kept in Dubrovnik. The document, dated 3 July 1281, describes vexillum unum de zendato rubeo et blavo — a flag made of red and blue silk. Its actual arrangement is unknown, so the familiar horizontal red-and-blue bicolour is a modern reconstruction, not a confirmed surviving design.

Flag of the Serbian Empire (14th century)

During the reign of Emperor Stefan Dušan (1346–1355), Serbian imperial heraldry prominently featured the double-headed eagle. A 1339 portolan chart by Angelino Dulcert, created while Dušan was still king, depicts a flag with a red double-headed eagle on a yellow field. Other standards attributed to Dušan include a purple imperial banner with a golden cross and a red-and-yellow cavalry flag. The precise appearance of a single official imperial flag is uncertain, and the 1339 depiction technically predates the empire's establishment.

Flag of the Principality of Serbia (1835)

Introduced under the Sretenje Constitution of 15 February 1835, this flag featured three horizontal stripes of red, white and blue, with the Serbian coat of arms in the centre. The emblem consisted of a white cross and four firesteels on a red shield, surrounded by oak and olive branches. The constitution was suspended shortly afterwards under pressure from foreign powers, making this a short-lived design.

Flag of the Principality of Serbia (1839–1878)

A special Ottoman firman issued in January 1839 established a new horizontal tricolour, with red at the top, blue in the middle and white at the bottom. The Serbian coat of arms appeared on the central blue stripe, while four six-pointed stars were placed in the upper-left portion of the red stripe. The stars symbolised the Ottoman Sultan's suzerainty over the autonomous principality.

Flag of the Principality of Serbia (1878–1882)

Following international recognition of Serbian independence at the Congress of Berlin in 1878, the four stars representing Ottoman authority were removed. The red-blue-white horizontal tricolour and the Serbian coat of arms were retained. This design represented Serbia as an independent principality until its elevation to kingdom status in 1882.

Flag of the Kingdom of Serbia (1882–1918)

Following the proclamation of the Kingdom of Serbia in 1882, the national tricolour was retained, but the coat of arms was replaced with the newly adopted royal arms. These featured a white double-headed eagle on a red shield, with the Serbian cross and four firesteels on a smaller shield at its breast, beneath a royal crown. Two versions existed: a state flag displaying the royal coat of arms and a plain tricolour used as the civil flag.

Flag of the Federated State of Serbia / People's Republic of Serbia (1945–1947)

Following the Second World War, Serbia became a constituent state of the Democratic Federal Yugoslavia. Its flag consisted of the traditional red-blue-white horizontal tricolour, with a red five-pointed star placed in the centre of the blue stripe. The star represented the communist-led Partisan movement and the new socialist political order.

Flag of the People's Republic of Serbia / Socialist Republic of Serbia (1947–1992)

In 1947, the flag was modified by moving the red five-pointed star to the centre of the entire tricolour and surrounding it with a golden border. The red-blue-white stripes were retained. The flag continued in use when the republic was renamed the Socialist Republic of Serbia in 1963, remaining its official flag throughout the socialist Yugoslav period until the early 1990s.

Flag of the Republic of Serbia (1992–2006)

After the breakup of socialist Yugoslavia, Serbia retained the traditional red-blue-white tricolour but removed the communist red star. The plain flag represented Serbia within the Federal Republic of Yugoslavia and subsequently the State Union of Serbia and Montenegro. In 2004, a parliamentary recommendation introduced a separate state flag displaying the coat of arms, although the plain tricolour continued as the civil and national flag.

Flag of the Republic of Serbia (2006–present)

Following the dissolution of the State Union of Serbia and Montenegro in 2006, Serbia continued using the red-blue-white horizontal tricolour. The plain version is the national or civil flag, while a separate state version carries the national coat of arms. The 2009 law on national symbols gave legal force to the designs introduced in 2004, and the 2010 regulations standardised their colours and heraldic details.

State flag of the Republic of Serbia (2010–present)

The state flag consists of three horizontal stripes of red, blue and white, with the lesser coat of arms positioned slightly toward the hoist and overlapping the three stripes. The coat of arms features a crowned white double-headed eagle, with a red shield bearing a white cross and four firesteels on its breast. Its modern graphic specifications were approved by the Serbian government on 11 November 2010. The plain tricolour remains the national or civil flag, so the two versions serve distinct official purposes.

Historical note

Serbia's modern national colours are red, blue and white, first formally established in the nineteenth century. The tricolour has remained remarkably consistent since 1839, apart from the addition of the royal coat of arms in 1882 and the communist red star during the socialist period. The double-headed eagle and the cross with four firesteels connect the modern coat of arms to Serbia's medieval dynastic and religious heraldry.

Historical clarification

  • The 1281 flag is known only from a written inventory. Its colours are documented, but its precise arrangement is not. The commonly illustrated red-and-blue horizontal version is hypothetical.
  • The Serbian Empire had several associated standards. The yellow flag with a red double-headed eagle is based on the 1339 portolan chart, which predates Dušan's imperial coronation in 1346. The purple banner with a golden cross is another standard traditionally attributed to him.
  • The 1835 and 1839 flags had different colour orders. The 1835 constitutional design was red-white-blue, while the 1839 design established the familiar red-blue-white arrangement.
  • The four stars on the 1839 flag represented Ottoman suzerainty. They were removed after Serbia gained independence in 1878.
  • The Kingdom of Serbia had two flag versions from 1882: a plain civil tricolour and a state flag bearing the royal coat of arms.
  • The 1945–1947 and 1947–1992 socialist flags differed in star placement. The earlier version placed the star on the blue stripe, while the later version placed it in the centre of the entire flag.
  • Modern Serbia has both a national/civil flag and a state flag. The former is the plain tricolour; the latter includes the coat of arms. The 2010 standardisation refined the appearance of both rather than introducing entirely new designs.
