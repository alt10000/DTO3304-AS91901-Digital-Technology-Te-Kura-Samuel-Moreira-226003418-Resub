Flag of Andorra (1806–1866)

Tradition describes a vertical yellow-and-red bicolour, taken from the colours of the Counts of Foix, who shared rule of Andorra with the Bishop of Urgell. No contemporary document proves it was ever flown, so it is treated as traditional rather than confirmed.

Flag of Andorra (1866–1939)

Introduced during the New Reform of 1866, this flag featured three vertical stripes of blue, yellow and red, traditionally attributed to Napoleon III. The colours represented Andorra's historical connections with France and Spain, and the design established the basis of the modern Andorran flag.

Flag of Andorra (1939–1949)

Around 1939 Andorra’s blue-yellow-red tricolour gained a coat of arms on the yellow stripe, showing the symbols of the Bishop of Urgell, the Counts of Foix, Catalonia and Béarn. The colours reflect the country’s ties to its two co-princes, the French head of state and the Spanish bishop. This early version had a crown but not yet the motto Virtus Unita Fortior.

Flag of Andorra (1949–1959)

From about 1949 a redrawn coat of arms sat on the yellow stripe of the tricolour, still showing the four quarters that stand for Andorra’s historic rulers and neighbours. Because no law fixed the design, the proportions and heraldic details still varied from flag to flag.

Flag of Andorra (1959–1971)

By 1959 the arms had been redrawn again, and flags were still made in differing shades and proportions. The blue and red recall France and the yellow and red recall Spain and Foix, matching Andorra’s French and Spanish co-princes. The design was settled further with the 1971 version.

Modern Flag of Andorra (1971–present)

Introduced in 1971, the modern flag features three vertical stripes of blue, yellow and red, with the national coat of arms centred on the yellow stripe. The design was formally standardised in the 1993 Constitution, following Andorra's independence as a sovereign parliamentary co-principality and its admission to the United Nations.

Historical note: The dates 1949–1959 and 1959–1971 refer to different illustrated versions of the flag rather than clearly documented official adoptions. The precise historical development of these designs is somewhat uncertain.
