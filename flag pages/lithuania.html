Lithuania

Historical Flags

Lithuania's flag history includes medieval heraldic banners, the short-lived German-backed Kingdom of Lithuania, several Soviet-era designs, and the restoration of the traditional yellow-green-red tricolour in 1988.

Royal banner of the Grand Duchy of Lithuania (15th–18th centuries)

The traditional royal and state banner featured a white armoured knight on a white horse, holding a raised sword, against a red field. Known as Vytis (the Pursuer), the mounted knight became the principal heraldic symbol of Lithuania. Historical accounts describe red banners at the Battle of Grunwald in 1410, including thirty bearing the mounted knight and ten bearing the Columns of the Gediminids.

Supposed appearance of the royal (military) banner of the Grand Duchy of Lithuania (15th century reconstruction)

This depiction represents the early military banners associated with the Lithuanian grand dukes. The earliest surviving illustration, from a manuscript completed in 1443, shows a red banner with a mounted knight. Some reconstructions portray the knight carrying a lance rather than the raised sword familiar from later versions. The exact appearance of every medieval banner cannot be established from the surviving evidence.

Royal banner of Poland–Lithuania (16th–18th centuries)

Following the dynastic union of Poland and Lithuania, and particularly the establishment of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth in 1569, royal banners increasingly combined the heraldic symbols of both states. The principal design featured the Polish White Eagle and the Lithuanian Vytis, often arranged in alternating quarters on a white field. Some later versions incorporated the personal or dynastic coat of arms of the reigning monarch. Separate Polish and Lithuanian banners continued to be used for certain royal ceremonies.

Flag of the Kingdom of Lithuania (1918)

The German-backed Kingdom of Lithuania was briefly proclaimed in July 1918, with Prince Wilhelm of Urach selected to become King Mindaugas II. No distinct flag was created for the proposed monarchy; the yellow-green-red horizontal tricolour, approved earlier that year by the Council of Lithuania, served as the country's national flag. The kingdom project ended following Germany's defeat in the First World War.

State flag and civil ensign of the Republic of Lithuania (1918–1940)

The independent Lithuanian Republic adopted a horizontal tricolour of yellow, green and red, with three equal stripes. The design was approved on 19 April 1918, following the work of a commission comprising Jonas Basanavičius, Antanas Žmuidzinavičius and Tadas Daugirdas. Yellow represented the sun and prosperity, green the country's countryside and hope, and red the courage and sacrifice of those who fought for Lithuania.

Soviet puppet state (1918–1919)

The short-lived Lithuanian Soviet Socialist Republic and the subsequent Socialist Soviet Republic of Lithuania and Belorussia (Litbel) used a plain red flag. The design reflected their Bolshevik allegiance and did not include Lithuania's traditional heraldic symbols. These Soviet-backed governments collapsed in 1919 during the Lithuanian Wars of Independence.

Flag of the Lithuanian Soviet Socialist Republic (1940–1941)

Following the Soviet occupation of Lithuania in June 1940, the traditional tricolour was replaced with a red flag. Its upper-left corner featured the gold inscription LIETUVOS TSR (Lithuanian SSR), with a gold hammer and sickle beneath it. This design was introduced on 30 July 1940 and used until the German invasion of the Soviet Union in June 1941.

Flag of the Lithuanian Soviet Socialist Republic (1944–1953)

After Soviet forces reoccupied Lithuania in 1944, the Soviet authorities restored the red flag used by the Lithuanian SSR. It featured the gold abbreviation LTSR, representing the Lithuanian Soviet Socialist Republic, and the hammer and sickle in the upper-left corner. It remained in use until the adoption of a redesigned flag in 1953.

Flag of the Lithuanian Soviet Socialist Republic (1953–1988)

Adopted on 15 July 1953, this design consisted of a red field with a gold hammer and sickle and a gold-bordered red star in the upper-left corner. Two horizontal stripes were added along the bottom: a narrow white stripe above a wider green stripe. The reverse side omitted the hammer, sickle and star, leaving the red, white and green design.

National flag and civil ensign of Lithuania (1988–2004)

During the Lithuanian National Revival, the traditional yellow-green-red tricolour was officially restored on 18 November 1988, when the Lithuanian Soviet Socialist Republic recognised it as the state's flag. It was ceremonially raised over Gediminas Tower in Vilnius on 7 October 1988. The design was the same horizontal tricolour used by independent Lithuania between 1918 and 1940.

National flag and civil ensign of Lithuania (2004–present)

The modern flag retains the yellow, green and red horizontal tricolour, with equal-sized stripes. The 2004 amendments to the national flag legislation established its current proportions of 3:5, replacing the 1:2 proportions used during the earlier restoration period. Yellow represents the sun and prosperity, green the country's landscape and hope, and red the courage and sacrifice of the Lithuanian people.
