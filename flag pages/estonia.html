Flag of the Estonian Soviet Socialist Republic (1940–1953)

Adopted on 31 October 1940 following the Soviet occupation and annexation of Estonia. It consisted of a red field with a golden hammer and sickle in the upper-left corner, with the gold abbreviation ENSV (Eesti Nõukogude Sotsialistlik Vabariik) above them.

Flag of the Estonian Soviet Socialist Republic (1953–1990)

Adopted on 6 February 1953, this redesigned flag featured a red field with a golden hammer and sickle and a red star outlined in gold. Near the bottom were blue and white wavy stripes, representing the Baltic Sea; the design was created by Estonian artist Paul Luhtein.

Flag of Estonia (1990–present)

The blue-black-white horizontal tricolour was officially restored as Estonia's state flag on 7 August 1990, following decades of Soviet prohibition. Originally consecrated on 4 June 1884 as the flag of the Estonian University Student Association, it became a national symbol and was first adopted as the state flag in 1918. The blue represents the sky and hope, black is associated with the homeland and the traditional clothing of Estonians, and white symbolises purity and the aspiration for a brighter future.

Historical note: Estonia's blue-black-white flag was raised again over Tallinn's Pikk Hermann Tower on 24 February 1989, before the formal restoration of independence on 20 August 1991. The Soviet-era flag was officially discontinued in May 1990.
