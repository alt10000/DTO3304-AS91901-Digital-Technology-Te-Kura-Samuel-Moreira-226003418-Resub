Historical Flags

Vexilloid of Great Moravia (9th–10th centuries)

No reliably documented national flag or military standard of Great Moravia has survived. Modern depictions of its vexilloid are therefore reconstructions, often based on later Slavic heraldic traditions rather than confirmed contemporary evidence.

Banner of the Duchy of Bohemia (12th century)

A medieval banner associated with the Přemyslid dynasty, featuring a black eagle, generally depicted on a yellow or gold field. The eagle was an early heraldic symbol of the Bohemian rulers, before the double-tailed lion became the kingdom's principal emblem.

Banner of the Kingdom of Bohemia (14th century)

A red banner bearing a silver (white) double-tailed lion, the heraldic emblem of the Kingdom of Bohemia. The lion became closely associated with Bohemian royal authority and remains an important part of the Czech national coat of arms.

Flag of Czechoslovakia (1918–1920)

The first flag of independent Czechoslovakia was a simple horizontal white-and-red bicolour, based on the traditional colours of Bohemia. It was replaced in 1920 by the familiar design with a blue triangle at the hoist, which distinguished it from Poland's similar flag.

Flag of the Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia (1939–1945)

During the German occupation, the Protectorate used a horizontal tricolour of white, red and blue. The design resembled the Czechoslovak national colours, but the independent Czechoslovak flag with its blue hoist triangle was prohibited under Nazi rule.

Flag of the Czech Republic (1993–present)

Adopted on 1 January 1993 following the dissolution of Czechoslovakia, this flag retained the former Czechoslovak design: two horizontal bands of white and red with a blue triangle extending from the hoist. Although originally created for Czechoslovakia in 1920, the Czech Republic continued using it as its national flag after independence.

Historical note: The Czech flag's colours derive from Bohemian heraldry: white represents the silver lion and red its shield. Blue was added in 1920 to distinguish the Czechoslovak flag from Poland's white-and-red flag, and also came to represent Slovakia and Moravia.
